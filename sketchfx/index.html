    How it works

    Your strokes are cut wherever they turn back in the x direction. Steep, near-vertical parts are left out, since x = c is not the graph of a function.

    Each part is then matched against a few families: constant, line, parabola, cubic, circle arc, sine wave and exponential. When none of them stays close enough, the part is split at its sharpest corner (or where the fit is worst) and both halves are tried again. Neighbours that one formula can cover are joined back together, and the numbers are rounded as far as the fit allows.

    If two parts sit over the same x, they cannot belong to one function, so they go into f1, f2, and so on, each with its own domain. A circle, for example, comes out as an upper and a lower half.

    Tap or click a curve to see which piece it is; tapping a row in the Pieces list does the same in the other direction.

    The Fit slider sets how far the graph may stray from your strokes, measured against the size of the drawing. Distance is measured across the curve, not straight up and down.

    D draw, E erase, P move, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z redo. Scroll or pinch to zoom; right-drag or drag with two fingers to move.

    With a pen (Apple Pencil and the like), the pen draws and a finger moves the view; a hand resting on the screen is ignored while the pen is down.
